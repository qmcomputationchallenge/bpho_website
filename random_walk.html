Random Walk

The random walk can be mathematically modeled using random variables. Let an idealized point particle travelling in two dimensions be a distance \(X_n\) from the origin after n time steps. Every time step, we add a vector \((S \cos(\theta_i), S \sin(\theta_i))\) to its position vector, which starts at \((0,0)\). Here, S is the fixed distance travelled every step and \(\theta_i\) is a random angle between the horizontal axis and the direction of motion at the fith step. Our aim is to find the expectation of \(X_n\). \(X_n\) can be computed by finding the magnitude of the position vector: \[X_n = \lvert (S \cos(\theta_1), S \sin(\theta_1)) + (S \cos(\theta_2), S \sin(\theta_2)) + ... + (S \cos(\theta_n), S \sin(\theta_n))\rvert\] \[= S\sqrt{(\cos(\theta_1)+ ... + \cos(\theta_n))^2 + (\sin(\theta_1)+ ... + \sin(\theta_n))^2}\] Note that graphically \(E(\cos(\theta_i))\) and \(E(\sin(\theta_i))\) are both zero since they have zero signed area underneath them from \(0\) to \(2 \pi\). \[E(\cos^2(\theta_i)) = 1/2\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi} \cos^2(\theta_i)\, d\theta_i = 1/4\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi} \cos(2\theta_i)\ + 1d\theta_i = 1/2 \] \[E(\sin^2(\theta_i)) = 1/2\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi} \sin^2(\theta_i)\, d\theta_i = 1/4\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi} -\cos(2\theta_i)\ + 1d\theta_i = 1/2\] The square of the sum in the expression for \(X_n\) equals a sum of the squares of cosines and sines assuming that the random angle at each step is independent of the random angles at other steps. This gives: \[ E(X_n^2) = E(S^2((\cos(\theta_1)+ ... + \cos(\theta_n))^2 + ((\sin(\theta_1)+ ... + \sin(\theta_n)^2) \] \[ E(X_n^2) = E(S^2((\cos(\theta_1)+ ... + \cos(\theta_n))^2 + ((\sin(\theta_1)+ ... + \sin(\theta_n)^2) \] \[ E(X_n^2) = E(S\sqrt{\cos(\theta_1)+ ... + \cos(\theta_n))^2 + ((\sin(\theta_1)+ ... + \sin(\theta_n)^2}) \] \[= E(S^2\cos^2(\theta_1)+ ... + \cos^2(\theta_n) + \sin^2(\theta_1)+ ... + \sin^2(\theta_n))\] \[ = S^2(1/2 + 1/2 .... +1/2) = S^2n \] Thus we obtain the root mean square of the distance travelled after n steps of length S as \(S\sqrt{n}\)
