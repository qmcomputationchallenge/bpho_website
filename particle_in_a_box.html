Particle in a Box

The Schrodinger equation

We can obtain the schrodinger equation by promoting classical observables like position, momentum and energy with quantum operators which will act on the wavefunction, \(\psi\) to given eigenvalues corresponding to observed values. The operators are defined: \[\hat{P} = -i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \] \[\hat{H} = i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \] Where \(\hat{P}\) is the momentum operator and \(\hat{H}\) the hamiltonian, whose eigenvalues are total energy \(E\)\. Here we consider one dimension of time and space but in three dimensions derivatives of \(y\) and \(z\) are included in the momentum. In classical mechanics: \[E = T +V = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 +V = \frac{p^2}{2m} + V\] Using our new operators: \[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} +V\psi= i\hbar\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = E\psi\]

The infinite square well

The infinite square well has a potential: \[ V = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \infty & x\le 0 & a\ge x \\ 0 & 0\leq x \leq a \\ \end{array} \right. \] Thus inside the well \(V = 0\) so the schrodinger equation reads: \[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\Psi}{\partial x^2} = i\hbar\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial t} = E\Psi\] With the added constraint that \(\psi(0) = \psi(a) = 0\) since beyond this region the infinite potential naturally makes \(\psi\) vanish. Our aim is to look for separable solutions \(\Psi(x,t) = \psi(x)\eta(t)\)\ \[\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = -\frac{2mE}{\hbar^2}\psi\] looks exactly like the equation \[\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = -\omega^2y\] met in classical simple harmonic motion (SHM). The same equation means the same solution and so without integrating anything we can write: \[\psi(x) = A\sin(k t - \phi)\] for some constants, \(A\), \(\phi\) and \(k = \sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar}} \) To solve the rest of the problem we must find the constants \(A\) and \(\phi\) and also find the time dependence of \(\Psi(x,t)\)\, the wave-function of space and time. Recall \(\psi(0) = 0\)\. Since \(\sin(0) = 0\) we must have \(\phi = \pi n\)\. Interestingly we can actually just set \(\phi = 0\) here because if it is an even integer of \(\pi\) our wavefunction will be the same and if it is an odd integer of \(\pi\) the wavefunction will have a different sign, which does not matter since only the wavefunction's modulus squared will give us probabilities. To find A we will "normalise" the wavefunction, which means ensuring that the total probability of finding a particle in the well is 1 as espected. Mathematically: \[\int_0^a\psi^2dx =1 \] \[A^2\int_0^a\sin^2(k x)dx = 1 \] \[A^2\left[\frac{kx}{2k} + \frac{\sin2x}{4k}\right]_0^a\] \[\frac{A^2a}{2} = 1\] Now recall \(\psi(a) = 0\) so since \(\sin(n\pi) = 0 \), \(ka = n\pi\). \[ A = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\] Thus we have: \[\psi(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin(k x)\] where the condition on \(k\) also gives us a discrete set of energies \(E\) we will label \(E_n\): \[2mE_n/\hbar^2 = k^2 = n^2\pi^2\] \[E_n = \frac{\hbar^2\pi^2n^2}{2m}\] All that is left is to find time dependence. If we put subscripts on \(k\) like with energy so that \(k_n = n\pi/a\) and also on our wavefunctions: \[\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin(k_nx)\] we can write for \(\Psi_n(x,t) = \psi_n(x) \eta_n(t)\)\ \[i\hbar\frac{\partial \Psi_n(x,t)}{\partial t} = E_n \Psi_n(x,t)\] since the spatial part \(\psi\) can be taken out and cancelled. \[i\hbar\frac{\partial \eta_n(t)}{\partial t} = E_n \eta(t)\] so \[\eta(t) =\eta(0)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar} \] \[\Psi(x,t) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin(k_nx)\eta(0)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar} \] Note that the complex conjugate of \(e^{i\varphi}\) is \(e^{-i\varphi}\) and they both multiply to one. Thus, \(\eta(0) = 1\) to maintain normalisation. This has the interesting consequence of probabilities being time independent for single energy states: non trivial time dependence occurs if we take linear combinations of these states, which are equally valid solutions. \[\Psi(x,t) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{a}}\sin(k_nx)e^{-iE_nt/\hbar} \]

Demonstrating the uncertainty principle

First we calculate (\langle x\rangle \) and \(\langle x^2\rangle\) \[\langle x\rangle = \int_0^ax\psi^2dx\] \[\langle x\rangle = \int_0^a\frac{2}{a}x\sin^2kxdx\] We use integration by parts: \[\frac{a\langle x\rangle}{2} = \left[\frac{x^2}{2} -\frac{\sin(2kx)}{4k}\right]_0^a - \int_0^a\frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin(2kx)}{4k} dx \] \[\frac{a\langle x\rangle}{2} = \left[\frac{x^2}{2} -\frac{\sin(2kx)}{4k} - \frac{x^2}{4} +\frac{\cos(2kx)}{8k^2} dx \right]_0^a\] recall that \(ka = n\pi\) and that \(\cos2\pi n = 1\) and \(\sin2\pi n = 0\) so: \[\langle x\rangle = \frac{2}{a}\left[\frac{a^2}{2}-\frac{a^2}{4}\right] =\frac{a}{2} \] \[\langle x^2\rangle = \int_0^a\frac{2}{a}x^2\sin^2kxdx \] \[= \frac{2}{a}\left[ \frac{x^3}{2} - \frac{x^2 \sin2kx }{4k}-\frac{x^3}{2} - \frac{x\cos2kx}{4k^2}+\frac{x^3}{6}+\frac{\sin2kx}{8k^3}\right]_0^a \] \[= \ a^2(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{2 n^2 \pi^2})\] The standard deviation will be: \[\sigma_x = \sqrt{\langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2}\] \[ = a \sqrt{\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}}\] Now for momentum: \[\langle p\rangle = 0\] and from the de-Broglie wavelength relation \(p = h/\lambda = \hbar k\) so: \[\langle p^2\rangle = \hbar^2 k^2 = \frac{\hbar^2n^2\pi^2}{a^2}\] Finally Heisenberg's uncertainty principle can be tested: \[\sigma_p\sigma_x = \hbar n \pi \sqrt{\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}}\] \[ = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n^2 \pi^2}{3} - 2}\] the minimum uncertainty is in the ground state n = 1 so: \[Min(\sigma_p\sigma_x) = \frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi^2-6}{3}}\] But \(\pi>3\) and so \(\pi^2>9)\ meaning \( \frac{\pi^2-6}{3}>1)\ and finally \[\sigma_p\sigma_x > \frac{\hbar}{2}\]
