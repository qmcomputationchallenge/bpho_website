Electron Diffraction

In the electron diffraction experiment, electrons are emitted via thermionic emission from a current carrying wire and are incident on a crystal, for example graphite. The aim is to determine the atomic spacing by analysing the circles of constructive intereference seen on a phosphor screen due to the diffracted electrons. If the electrons are accelerated through a potential $V$ the conservation of energy gives: \[eV = \frac{1}{2}mv^2\] De-Broglie's formula for the wavelength of a 'matter wave' used to model the wave-like behavior of particles is: \[p = \frac{h}{\lambda}\] Thus since \(p = mv\) by definition the electron's 'wavelength' is: \[\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}\] Bragg's law relates this wavelength to the atomic spacing \(d\). If waves are incident at angle \(\theta\) to atoms in a grid along the horizontal the path difference is \(2d\sin \theta\) and the condition for constructive interference is: \[2d\sin\theta = n\lambda\] where \(n\) is any integer. Since the electrons are incident along the horizontal in the electron diffraction experiment, they will be an angle \(\phi = 2\theta\) when diffracted towards the rings of constructive interference, an angle that can easily be measured by measuring \(\tan\phi\) using a vernier caliper or on an image. Thus the atomic spacing can be calculated from: \[d = \frac{nh\ }{2\sqrt{2meV} \sin \frac{\phi}{2}}
